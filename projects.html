  • MS Excel-VBA

The module benchmarked two companies side-by-side, generated B–factor comparisons, highlighted performance gaps, and produced an automated summary report covering risk, capital structure, operational health, and financial stability. Amplitude Modulation

  • Wolfram Mathematica
  • Investigated the full theoretical pipeline of amplitude modulation: carrier generation, modulating waveform synthesis, single-tone AM formation, and extension to multi-frequency audio signals.
  • Implemented AM mathematically using y(t) = [A + M·cos(ωmt + ϕ)]·sin(ωct), focusing on the interaction between baseband and high-frequency carriers.
  • Applied the forward Fourier Transform to decompose the AM signal into spectral lines—carrier, upper sideband, lower sideband—and analytically derived sideband amplitudes under different modulation indices.
  • Used the inverse Fourier Transform to reconstruct the original AM waveform, confirming theoretical spectral symmetry and linear composition of sinusoidal components.

Time Series Simulation & ARIMA Forecasting

  • Stata
  • Python

Conducted extensive time-series simulations, generating between 10 and 10,000 realizations of Random Walk, AR(1), AR(2), MA(1), and MA(2) processes to analyse convergence behaviour, oscillation patterns, and long-run variance stability. To isolate the stochastic component of each series, I applied detrending and differencing transformations and validated stationarity using DF, ADF, and KPSS testing frameworks. I further compared linear, quadratic, and cubic deterministic trend models using AIC/BIC, empirically establishing the linear trend as the optimal specification. Using these diagnostics, I estimated multiple ARIMA configurations—(0,1,1), (1,1,0), (1,1,1), (2,1,0), and (0,1,2)—and selected the best-fit model through information criteria and residual analysis. The project also included a complete forecasting pipeline, incorporating PACF/ACF interpretation, residual validation, and visualisation of out-of-sample predictions.

Behavioural Insights from WHO Road Safety Campaign

  • Power BI
  • Excel

An advanced Power BI dashboard to analyse ten years of qualitative and behavioural data from WHO’s Road Safety Campaign conducted across 9 major Mexican cities. The project involved studying household- and community-level behavioural responses, perception shifts, and campaign effectiveness over time. Using Power BI’s data-modelling and interactive-visualisation capabilities, I transformed raw qualitative inputs into structured behavioural indicators—attitude shifts, risk perceptions, compliance patterns, and demographic-based differences. The dashboard features drill-down city-level comparisons, behavioural segmentation, temporal trend analysis, and clustering of households based on safety awareness and policy responsiveness, measuring which campaign messages resulted in measurable shifts in public awareness. Final insights and visual reports were prepared for policy partners, enabling evidence-based refinement of future road-safety interventions.
