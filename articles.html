M.A. dissertation · Delhi Technological University

Impact of CSR & SDG initiatives on IT sector

This study examines how sustainability initiatives—specifically Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) spending and alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)—affect the long-term financial performance of Indian IT firms. While global development agendas emphasise responsible corporate behaviour, India’s mandatory CSR policy offers a unique opportunity to study how such obligations translate into measurable economic and social returns.

To address the gap between sustainability disclosures and measurable financial outcomes, I created a primary dataset by collecting, standardizing, and coding CSR and SDG-related disclosures from annual reports, sustainability filings, EXIM datasets, and financial statements. This resulted in a consolidated dataset of 1,500+ data points combining financial ratios, R&D expenditure, export-import data, CSR spending patterns, and non-financial corporate disclosures.

Using this dataset, I evaluated CSR–SDG financial efficiency through multi-variable regression models, linking sustainability indicators to key financial metrics such as Return on Investment (ROI) and Return on Total Assets (ROTA). I also developed a novel scoring metric, the C-SDG Index, to quantify the alignment between a firm’s CSR activities and the 17 UN SDGs—transforming qualitative ESG data into measurable strategic signals.

Development and Poverty Disparities: A Comparative Analysis of Bihar and Nigeria

I conducted primary field research, designing and administering my own household questionnaire to capture updated deprivation indicators across education, health, and living standards. To estimate current poverty and development levels, I compared India’s 2011 MPI and HDI datasets with projected values for 2024 using a multi-method approach. This included applying the Rangarajan Committee (2014) poverty methodology, incorporating Suresh Tendulkar’s (2005) consumption norms, and adjusting all poverty and consumption indicators using inflation-corrected MPCE indices.

This integrated framework allowed me to reconstruct and update multidimensional poverty estimates and evaluate changes in human development over time.

Closing loopholes: Real-time digital media monitoring using AI-tools to counter digital tobacco marketing (opens in a new tab)

Published in Tobacco Induced Diseases (JCI: Q2, IF: 2.8), 2025 special issue

World Conference on Tobacco Control 2025 · Tob. Induc. Dis. 2025;23(Suppl 1):A55

AuthorsNandita Murukutla, Melina Magsumbol, Anand Ankit, Ashish Gupta, Stephen Hamill, Sandra Mullin

In this study, I worked as part of the core research team that developed and deployed a real-time AI-based digital media monitoring system (TERM) to detect covert tobacco marketing across India, Indonesia, Mexico, and cross-border platforms; in 2023 it documented 41,598 marketing instances from 308 accounts.
